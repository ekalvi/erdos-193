An infinite walk inN16, using only unit steps, with no
three collinear points

School of Computer Science

Abstract
Is it possible to walk to infinity, avoiding three collinear points, using as steps only
the standard unit basis vectors(0, 0, . . . ,0, 1, 0, . . . ,0)? We prove this is possible in16
dimensions.
1 Introduction
Let k≥ 2, and consider an infinite walk inNk using only steps that are basis vectors of
the form(0, 0,..., 0, 1, 0,..., 0). For whichk can one avoid some finite number of collinear
points?
For dimensionk = 2every such infinite planar walk must have every possible finite number
of collinear points. This is the content of the famous Gerver-Ramsey theorem [2, 10, 6]. Also
see [7].
For dimensionk = 3Gerver and Ramsey proved that there is an infinite walk that avoids
511 + 1collinear points. This was recently improved to189by Lidbetter [9].
Of course it is impossible to avoid2collinear points in such a walk. This raises the natural
question: for which dimensionsk can one avoid three collinear points? One cannot do it for
k = 3, as shown by Brown [2], since every walk of8unit steps contains three collinear points.
In this paper we solve the problem fork = 16. The construction can be given explicitly
as a morphism on16letters.
The description of such a walk can be given picturesquely as follows: if an infinitesimal

1.1 A reformulation
The problem of avoiding3collinear points can be rephrased in an equivalent way as follows,
which interprets the original problem in the terminology of combinatorics on words.
Consider an infinite sequence(an)n≥0 over a finite alphabetΣk ={0, 1,...,k− 1}, and
call itgoodif it contains no two consecutive nonempty blocksx,y (possibly of different sizes)
with the property that the|x|a/|x|=|y| a/|y|for all lettersa∈Σ k. Here|x|is the length of
the block and|x|a is the number of occurrences of a lettera.
The vectorψ(x) := (|x|0,|x| 1,...,|x| k−1)is often called theParikh vectorof the word
x∈{ 0, 1,...,k− 1}∗. The vector of letter frequenciesψ(x)/|x| is called the normalized
Parikh vector. Thus a good sequence has no two consecutive nonempty blocks with the same
normalized Parikh vector.
Equality of letter frequencies is sometimes calledweak abelian equivalence; see the discus-
sion in [1] and [5, Section 8.2]. Accordingly, the forbidden factorxy is sometimes termed
aweak abelian square. (An ordinaryabelian squareadditionally has|x| =|y| and consists
of two consecutive words, where the second is a permutation of the first. Avoiding ordinary
abelian squares alone is therefore not the whole requirement in this problem.)

is a small variation on theirs. Also see [8], which gave a lower bound on the length of the
longest good sequence in dimensionk(now made obsolete fork≥16).

5 The dimension bound and the proposed smaller mor-
phisms
Define kmin to be the least size of an alphabet admitting a good infinite word. Theorem 5
proves that this minimum exists and is at most16.
As mentioned above, we know thatkmin≥ 4. Therefore it is still of interest to determine
its exact value.
I found a number of candidate cyclic morphisms, but have been unable to prove that they
work. I list them here in the hope that they may aid other researchers to improve the bound
