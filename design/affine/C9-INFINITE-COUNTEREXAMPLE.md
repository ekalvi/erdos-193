# An infinite counterexample family for the coefficient-nine affine merge

## Status

**PROVED.**  The candidate

\[
R_n=W_n+9W_{2n}+81W_{5n}\in\mathbb Z^3
\]

contains infinitely many collinear triples.  This supersedes the clean
30,001-vertex prefix and closes `C=9` as a possible solution of Erdős #193.

## 1. Base counterexample

At indices

```text
191649, 191677, 191685
```

the exact vertices are

```text
(27958563, 26840581, 26460032)
(27962322, 26845887, 26462839)
(27963396, 26847403, 26463641).
```

The two adjacent chords are

```text
u = (3759,5306,2807) = 7(537,758,401),
v = (1074,1516,802) = 2(537,758,401).
```

Thus `2u=7v`, proving collinearity.

## 2. Closed base-7 form of the indices

Let

\[
i_0=191649,\qquad i_{k+1}=2401i_k+74000.
\]

In base seven, concatenation notation gives

\[
(i_k)_7=142\;5^{4k+2}\;13.                         \tag{2.1}
\]

For completeness, if `F(r)` is the value of `142 5^r 13`, then

\[
F(r)=79\,7^{r+2}+5\frac{7^r-1}{6}7^2+10.
\]

A direct subtraction gives

\[
F(r+4)-7^4F(r)
=(7^4-1)(5\cdot49/6-10)=74000,
\]

and `(i_0)_7=1425513`, proving (2.1) by induction.

Multiplication in base seven gives the two companion forms

```text
(2 i_k)_7 = 315 4^(4k+1) 326,
(5 i_k)_7 = 1110 1^(4k) 0501.                       (2.2)
```

These identities can also be checked by the same geometric-series formula.
The offsets needed below do not carry into the repeated runs:

```text
13_7  + [0,35]  lies in [13_7,63_7]    (two suffix digits),
326_7 + [0,71]  lies in [326_7,460_7]  (three suffix digits),
0501_7+[0,179]  lies in [0501_7,1145_7] (four suffix digits).
```

## 3. Exact four-digit automaton cycles

For the seven-uniform morphism, the fixed-point symbol is generated by the
base-7 DFA

\[
\lambda_n=\delta^*(0,(n)_7),\qquad
\delta(a,d)=\operatorname{MORPHISM}[a][d].
\]

After the three prefixes in (2.1)--(2.2), and after the initial number of
repeated digits, the states immediately before the fixed suffix are:

| multiplier | prefix | repeated digit | initial repetitions | state before suffix |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | `142` | 5 | 2 | 10 |
| 2 | `315` | 4 | 1 | 4 |
| 5 | `1110` | 1 | 0 | 4 |

Direct evaluation of the twelve-row morphism table gives

```text
delta_5^4(10)=10,
delta_4^4(4)=4,
delta_1^4(4)=4.                                      (3.1)
```

Equations (2.1), (2.2), the no-carry ranges, and (3.1) imply the all-`k`
factor identities

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda[i_k:i_k+36]&=\lambda[i_0:i_0+36],\\
\lambda[2i_k:2i_k+72]&=\lambda[2i_0:2i_0+72],\\
\lambda[5i_k:5i_k+180]&=\lambda[5i_0:5i_0+180].
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{3.2}
\]

This is the exact four-digit base-7 automaton cycle.  It is not an extrapolation
from a finite prefix.

## 4. Chord invariance and the infinite family

For `0<=h<=36`,

\[
R_{i_k+h}-R_{i_k}
=\Delta W(i_k,h)+9\Delta W(2i_k,2h)+81\Delta W(5i_k,5h),
\]

where `Delta W(a,l)` is the projected Parikh vector of the factor
`lambda[a:a+l]`.  Equation (3.2) makes this vector independent of `k` for every
such `h`.  In particular, the two adjacent chords at offsets `0,28,36` are the
same `u,v` displayed in Section 1 for every `k`.  Hence

\[
R_{i_k},\quad R_{i_k+28},\quad R_{i_k+36}
\]

are collinear for all `k>=0`.  Since `i_{k+1}>i_k`, these are infinitely many
bad triples.

## 5. Certificate and boundary

`c9_infinite_counterexample.py` independently embeds the morphism, evaluates
`W_n` in `O(log n)` exact arithmetic, verifies the base coordinate witness,
checks the digit-state cycles and suffix bounds, and regresses the first 64
members (whose indices already have hundreds of digits).  The finite depth is
not the proof; Sections 2--4 are the all-depth argument.

The depth-64 compact regression is
`c9-infinite-counterexample-summary.json` (SHA-256
`0936c2c933f211aee22cb56ca8b8d66b4c47dafc38de66c0e9ca7425095913db`).

This theorem refutes `C=9` only.  It does not establish a no-go theorem for all
weighted merges `W_n+C W_{2n}+C^2W_{5n}`.  Blind coefficient search remains
unjustified: any finite prefix excludes only finitely many coefficients.
