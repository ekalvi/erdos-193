2. Where g85 enters the math

In the explorer’s eight-sign numbering, 85 is binary 01010101: zero selects + and one selects −. That gives +−+−+−+−, repeated forever.

σ(n) = Σⱼ (−1)ʲ bⱼ(n) mod 4
uₙ = iσ(n)
zₙ = Σᵣ<ₙ uᵣ

Here bⱼ(n) is binary digit j, starting at the right. The alternating signs weight those digits. State 0 means east; 1 north; 2 west; 3 south.

If n ends in k binary ones, adding one changes the state by 1 when k is even, or 2 when k is odd, modulo four. This is why only eight transitions occur, rather than sixteen.

Rule 170 reverses all signs and produces a companion six-step construction. This page uses g85 only.

4. Why the six-coordinate walk inherits the proof

  1. The signed Gaussian argument gives a matching-endpoint-state identity: the power of 2 dividing |zₙ−zₘ|² equals the power dividing n−m.
  2. The offsets and height tags extend this to every pair: ν₂(|wₙ−wₘ|²)=ν₂(hₙ−hₘ).
  3. A collinear triple would force its positive height gaps A, B, and A+B to have the same power of 2. Impossible: after removing that common power, two odd integers have an even sum.
  4. The linear map T sending each coordinate direction to its vector in the table satisfies T(Pₙ)=Qₙ. Collinear P vertices would have collinear Q images. Those images are distinct because hₙ strictly increases.

This is a proof outline, not independent verification of the draft. In particular, the shifted-sign source identity must be checked mathematically; finite pictures do not prove it.

What ν₂ means

ν₂(12)=2 because 12=4×3; ν₂(10)=1. It counts the number of factors of two in a nonzero integer.

What this could advance

Smaller step sets in 3D

The table itself gives a 3D integer walk with only six allowed step vectors, instead of the original sixteen. The six-dimensional basis walk is its coordinate-count encoding.

Infinite words with unequal adjacent proportions

The A–F sequence avoids adjacent nonempty blocks having the same letter frequencies, even at unequal lengths. This is called weak abelian-square avoidance.

The minimum dimension

If the 6D proof is confirmed, L(d)=∞ for every d≥6 in the standard-basis path problem. The key remaining cases are d=4 and d=5; 3D is impossible for positive basis steps.

These are mathematical consequences, not established applications in cryptography, communication networks, or physical routing. Novelty relative to the broader literature still needs specialist review.
