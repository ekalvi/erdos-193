Two minima, unchanged bounds

s* counts one fixed integer step menu for an infinite no-three-collinear walk in three dimensions. d* is the positive-basis dimension minimum. Equality is not assumed.

Established: 4 ≤ d* ≤ s*.
Existing proposed ceiling: s* ≤ 6, with collaborator review pending.
No result in either round determines either minimum.

The original Erdős Problem 193 theorem of Cambie and Kalviainen is separate and unchanged. The six-step follow-up uses Kalviainen’s alternating source, Cambie’s offsets, and Shallit’s basis encoding. Shallit’s five-letter candidate is not refuted; partial fixed-ratio certificates are not an all-ratios proof.

What the competing tracks actually established

Analytic · restricted family

Gaussian tags: six remains optimal

Arbitrary scales, integer state tags, and infinite sign streams cannot beat six while preserving the stated all-pairs valuation certificate. The report classifies admissible tags and proves the collision obstruction.

Not: a universal six-step lower bound.

Deleting vertices: gaps ≤16 do not help

Every subsequence of the established walk with eventual gaps at most 16 requires six displacements. Independent JavaScript and C++ searches agree. All 1,048,575 phase/transition selectors also fail to beat six.

Alternate norms: several routes excluded

A ramified odd-prime norm with a fixed sum-free leading-unit certificate forces zero-density heights, incompatible with bounded positive height steps. One economical alternative source was just a coordinate change of the old one.

Not: exclusion of all odd-prime constructions.

The all-ratios probability sum has a sharp threshold. Independently, the interval-overlap graph and event marginals alone cannot certify infinite avoidance, even for iid bounded macroblocks.

Menus {a₁u,a₂u,a₃u,v,−v} have a sharp 15-step maximum. Sub-square-root deviation growth also excludes every rational 3D projection of a class of primitive uniform substitutions.

Analytic · specified Gaussian source

Each fixed menu is decidable

A finite return-language automaton and substitution matrix cycle give exact infinite YES/NO certificates. If any finite-menu subsequence exists, a 4-automatic selector exists with the same menu.

Not: a bound on selector complexity from menu size, or a uniform decision for all five-vector menus at once.

descent-automata.md

Exact obstructions · no changed minimum

Naive coarsening fails; its bad menus need not survive

A finite five-menu path gains a sixth vector under every fixed base-4 rounding offset. Yet a short, independently checked automaton certificate excludes that particular menu at infinity, even with gaps up to 27.

Not: an impossibility theorem for every gap-27 selector. A descent theorem restricted to infinitely extendable menus remains open.
