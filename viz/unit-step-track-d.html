The attempted shortcut

Jeffrey Shallit's candidate replaces each of five letters with a 14-letter pattern. Assign one fixed integer arrow in 3D to each letter. Could replacing letters with larger patterns simply transform the whole path by one fixed stretch, rotation, or shear, allowing an error that stays bounded?

1

Choose the arrows once. A rational 3-by-5 matrix A maps cumulative letter counts F(n) to Q(n).

2

Require one scaling law. For some fixed positive t and fixed real matrix B, Q(14tn) − BQ(n) must remain bounded for every n.

3

The written conclusion: all five columns of A coincide. Every step is the same arrow.

Why blocking or bounded errors do not rescue it

All substitution powers

A modular certificate at the prime 421 shows that the four rational frequency-deviation coordinates have no proper nonzero invariant subspace, for any positive substitution iterate.

Bounded-error rigidity

An integer sum-of-squares identity proves expansion. Any uniformly bounded error in this fixed linear scaling law must actually vanish.

The infinite claim is supplied by the written argument. The checker verifies its finite arithmetic inputs; it does not replace the proof with a prefix scan or a finite list of exponents.

What remains open
