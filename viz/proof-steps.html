The visual map before the algebra

Trace, select, and lift

The proof constructs one infinite walk with a finite step menu and no three collinear vertices. This page shows what each component does. It deliberately leaves the pair-law algebra to the guided proof.

The ordering that finally made the construction clear

The failed experiment lifts every Hilbert point. The successful construction instead traces the planar route, selects a controlled same-state subsequence, and only then lifts those selected points.

1. Trace a discrete Hilbert route

Start with a route through the 2D integer grid. At each larger size, four smaller copies are turned and joined so every consecutive route index reaches a neighboring grid point.

Call the point at index n by the name H(n). The index records when the route visits that floor position.

2. See what the tempting construction would do

If we lift every planar point by using its route index as height, H(0) gets height 0, H(1) gets height 1, and so on. The flat route becomes a rising 3D staircase.

3. Learn from the first failure

Lifting every point does not work: indices 3, 4, and 5 already produce three collinear vertices.

The three gold points lie on the red line in the diagram.

The final construction therefore needs a selector. It must remove the bad freedom without leaving unbounded gaps, because unbounded gaps could require infinitely many step vectors.

4. Select one same-state index from every block

The Hilbert decoder can finish in four orientation states, visualized as right, up, down, and left. These are four states—not sixteen colors or sixteen positions.

For each 16-index block, a fixed lookup chooses exactly one offset: 5 for incoming state I, 1 for S, 13 for T, or 3 for C. Its two base-4 digits cancel the incoming state, so every retained index finishes in state I.

Successive retained indices differ by 4 through 28. The white rings mark these fixed representatives; the selector does not scan for the first blue cell.

5. Give every selected pair one binary fingerprint

For two same-state indices, the first base-4 digit where they differ controls two things at once: the power of two in their index gap and a deliberately coarse fingerprint of their planar chord. The pair law says those two numbers are equal.

If three lifted points were collinear, their adjacent planar chords would be integer multiples rW and sW. The two adjacent pair laws make the coprime scale factors r and s odd, while the endpoint pair law makes r+s odd. Odd plus odd is even, so the assumed line cannot exist.

6. Bounded gaps give a finite step menu

The final lift uses only the selected points. Their height changes are the selected index gaps, so they lie between 4 and 28. Consecutive Hilbert points are floor neighbors, so a gap of at most 28 also bounds both planar coordinate changes.

Every 3D displacement therefore lies in one fixed finite box. The generated 500,000-step prefix realizes exactly the 16 arrows shown here; the theorem only needs finiteness, not minimality.

7. Separate the infinite proof from finite evidence

A program can test 500,000 steps—or a billion—and still not test forever. The Lean development checks the definitions, pair law, selector, no-three-collinear argument, Hilbert adjacency, and finite-menu conclusion for every natural-number position.

The finite artifact and exhaustive direction scan independently test the implementation. Neither is used as a premise of the infinite theorem. External mathematicians still need to review whether the formal definitions and theorem capture the intended problem exactly.
