Background: the key ideas

A primer on the concepts behind the construction and the current proof programme — the game itself, what’s known in two dimensions versus three, exact integer arithmetic, the enlarge-and-twist matrix, and the global connector-safety problem that remains. Every certified number is an exhaustively computed value from the project (github.com/ekalvi/erdos-193).

1 ·The concept: three properties, pick two

A curve of steps can be a smooth thread (dimension 1, not a crinkly fractal), it can be triple-free (no three points ever in a line), and it can use a finite menu of steps (bounded hops, as Erdős #193 demands). You can always get two — but the corner where all three meet appears to be empty. That empty corner is why the problem is hard, and it's why our object has to be a fractal thread.

How to read it. Each corner is a property you'd want; a curve that achieves two of them lives on the edge between those two corners and is pushed off the third. The bottom edge is drawn in blue because it's the pair Erdős #193 actually asks for — triple-free and a finite menu — and it doesn't even require dimension 1. Our construction is the flag planted on that blue edge: it nails both required properties, paying only with a slightly fractal dimension (1.10 instead of 1).

The same idea as a scorecard — five curves, ranked from furthest from the goal to closest. Green is a property met, red one missed; ties are broken by the simpler curve ranking as further out. Only the last row is all green.

2 ·The game: no three footprints on a line, ever

3 ·Two dimensions versus three: what is known

Erdős #193 — the Gerver–Ramsey problem — fixes a finite menu $S$ of allowed steps and asks whether an infinite walk, each step drawn from $S$, can avoid ever putting three of its points on a line. In the plane the answer is settled and negative: Gerver & Ramsey (1979) proved that for every finite $S\subset\mathbb{Z}^2$ and every $K$ there is a finite length $N(S,K)$ beyond which $K+1$ collinear points are forced. Taking $K=2$, no infinite planar walk can avoid three collinear points — the plane is simply too cramped. (In our own exhaustive check, the longest triple-free walk on the four unit steps $\pm e_1,\pm e_2$ has just 3 points.)

Three dimensions behave completely differently, which is why it is the setting this construction attacks. Gerver & Ramsey built an explicit infinite walk in $\mathbb{Z}^3$ that contains no $5^{11}+1 = 48{,}828{,}126$ collinear points — so, unlike the plane, a finite (if astronomically large) number of collinear points can be avoided forever. They conjectured their walk in fact never has more than three collinear points; in 2023 Lidbetter disproved that exact claim — the walk does contain six collinear points — but sharpened the bound to 189, so an infinite avoider is now known for every $k\ge 189$. What remains open is the pure “no three in a line” question: whether any finite menu admits an infinite $\mathbb{Z}^3$ walk with no three collinear points at all (even no four is unknown). That open 3D question is exactly the target of our $124$-step construction.

4 ·The machine: enlarge, twist, patch — repeat

5 ·Exact arithmetic on the integer lattice

Every footprint of the walk has whole-number coordinates, and every decision is made in exact integer arithmetic — no floating point ever touches a collinearity test, so there is no rounding to trust and nothing to drift over hundreds of thousands of points. Whether three points $p,q,r$ lie on a line is settled by a single integer quantity, the cross product $\Omega(p,q,r)=(q-p)\times(r-p)$: the three are collinear exactly when $\Omega$ is the zero vector. Because $\Omega$ is a triple of integers, “$=0$” is an exact yes-or-no, never a “close enough to zero.”

$$\Omega(p,q,r)=(q-p)\times(r-p),\qquad p,q,r\ \text{collinear}\iff \Omega=0.$$

The enlarging matrix $M$ has only integer entries, so $Mp$ is again a lattice point — the construction never leaves $\mathbb{Z}^3$. And enlargement meets the collinearity test through one clean identity, $\Omega(Mp,Mq,Mr)=\operatorname{cof}(M)\cdot\Omega(p,q,r)$, where $\operatorname{cof}(M)$ is the integer cofactor matrix. Since $\operatorname{cof}(M)$ is invertible, $\Omega$ vanishes after enlargement precisely when it vanished before: non-collinear triples stay non-collinear and collinear ones stay collinear. Triple-freeness is inherited exactly, with no error that could accumulate across scales.

6 ·One matrix that enlarges and twists

The engine of the construction is a single $3\times3$ integer matrix. Its determinant is $27$ and all three eigenvalues have modulus exactly $3$, so $M$ acts as a clean $\times 3$ enlargement — it blows the walk up by the same factor in every direction, with no direction stretched more than another. (In the right metric it is literally three times a rotation; see the next section.)

$$M=\begin{pmatrix}3&0&0\\0&0&-3\\0&3&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det M = 27,\qquad |\lambda_1|=|\lambda_2|=|\lambda_3|=3.$$

But $M$ does more than enlarge: it also twists. The rotation angle is $\arccos(-\tfrac16)\approx 99.6^\circ$, and because $-\tfrac16$ is not the cosine of any rational fraction of a full turn, that twist never repeats. Each enlargement sets the shape at a fresh tilt relative to every earlier copy, so no two scales ever line back up. This is the “imbrication,” or dishwasher, idea: the nested copies stack like bowls in a rack, not plates in a stack — and it is what stops far-apart parts of the walk from ever drifting into alignment.

7 ·Self-similar at every scale

Because $M$ is exactly an enlarge-and-rotate, the object it builds is a genuine self-similar fractal — more precisely a graph-directed self-similar set, in which every point spawns one enlarged copy of the whole walk (its anchor) plus a bounded set of stitched-in children. The self-similarity is exact, not approximate: there is an integer quadratic form $Q$ with $M^{\mathsf T}QM=9Q$, which says that in the $Q$-metric $M$ multiplies all distances by exactly $3$.

$$M^{\mathsf T}Q\,M = 9\,Q,\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&6&-1\\0&-1&6\end{pmatrix} \ \Longrightarrow\ d_Q(Mp,Mq)=3\,d_Q(p,q).$$

A self-similar curve has a fractal (box-counting) dimension $d=\log\lambda/\log 3$, where $\lambda\approx 3.36$ is the factor by which the point-count grows per enlargement; here $d\approx 1.10$. That is just above $1$ — the walk is a slightly crinkly thread, not a smooth line and not a space-filling blob. Self-similarity is also what makes a proof conceivable at all: since every scale is a faithful copy of the one beneath it, a single argument at one scale — that a fresh patch can always be stitched in without creating a collinear triple — can be lifted by induction to cover all infinitely many scales at once.

In 2D the plane is provably too cramped for this to continue (the level-2 stitches jam) — that's Gerver–Ramsey. In 3D, the identical machine with the 124-move menu ran eight rounds to a certified 311,738-point walk, and the open question is whether it provably runs forever.

10 ·The real obstruction: connector legality is global

Everything already placed remains safe when the whole level is enlarged, but each new connector point must be checked against every pair already in the prefix. A stitch can therefore jam without any local crowding: two remote old points can define a secant that passes through an otherwise clear candidate site. This is why the former “Lemma R” reduction to a uniformly lean thread was withdrawn. Local sparsity is useful geometry, but it is not an availability theorem.

The strongest finite guard so far. The quadratic direction ratio $J=(3y^2-yz+3z^2)/r^2$ is invariant under the enlarge-and-twist matrix. The guarded policy “grandfathers” the finite cone pairs already in its base, then refuses every connector that would create a new pair on $J=11/3$ or $J=348/275$. Starting from the independently certified guarded level 5, that policy completed all 8,295 level-6 stitches. A separate auditor checked all 28,804 points, all 414,820,806 point pairs, and every first selected word: no triple and no connector-born guarded-cone pair.

11 ·Where it stands now

  • Record and inheritance — a 311,738-point walk is exactly verified; invertible enlargement preserves every old non-collinearity. Proven / certified finite.
  • Consecutive guarded L5→L6 gate — all 8,295 stitches and 414,820,806 terminal pairs pass an independent audit. The same finite two-cone invariant is present at the output. Certified finite.
  • Complete choice landscape on that path — every one of 756,512,535 canonical word occurrences is classified; the exact floor is 71. Certified finite.
  • Two tempting shortcuts are closed — bounded phase/position cannot encode every fractional ghost contact, and the direct coefficient-9 affine merge has a proved infinite family of bad triples. Neither is an infinite construction.
  • Universal reachable-state safety — for every reachable prefix, every newly born, imported or silently returning exact line must enter a finite promoted class or have a strict escape rank; the remaining exact effects must still leave a connector whose successors satisfy the same invariant. Open.

In short: the project has a long verified record and a strong consecutive finite gate with substantial measured slack, but no unconditional solution of Erdős #193. The missing quantifier is “every reachable history,” not merely every prefix along one successful history.
