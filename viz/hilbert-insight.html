A Hilbert–2-adic insight, from maze to obstruction

An intuitive tour of a Hilbert-curve mechanism under evaluation. Move through the six scenes, drag the 3D view to orbit, and use the pair microscope to inspect the exact arithmetic rule on which the mechanism depends.

Status: research insight under exact evaluation. This mechanism is separate from the repository’s amplification programme. Hold this three-part mental model

The addressA base-4 number tells the Hilbert robot which nested quarter-square to enter at every scale.
The badgeTwo parity bits remember the final orientation of those nested, rotated maps. Equal badges let two addresses be rewound in sync.
The clockTime is used as height. A straight line would make its three gaps integer multiples a, b, and a+b of one direction.

The mechanism in one breath

  1. Hilbert gives small steps. The planar robot moves one square at a time; lifting time into height keeps a finite four-step menu before filtering.
  2. The colour synchronizes two addresses. Same-coloured base-4 indices finish in the same Hilbert orientation state.
  3. The first different digit measures scale exactly. One planar factor of two corresponds to two time factors of two, plus one bit determined only by direction.
  4. A line cannot satisfy that rule three times. Its pair lengths are a, b, and a+b; the rule would give all three the same number of factors of two, which is impossible.
  5. The chosen colour returns frequently. Every aligned 16-index block contains it, so retained jumps are at most 31 old steps and the new menu remains finite.

Where an adversarial review should push

The elementary a,b,a+b contradiction is the attractive part of the insight. The load-bearing unresolved point is the all-length Hilbert digit lemma shown in scene 4: equal colour must synchronize the reverse orientation state for every address length and indexing convention. Before this can become a proof route, the exact four-state Hilbert table and that lemma need a complete argument. The running finite audit tests the recorded prefix independently but cannot establish the infinite statement.
