Proof claim for Erdős Problem #193

Claim: The answer is no. There is a finite set S⊂Z^3 and an infinite S-walk with no three distinct collinear vertices.

Paper: A finite-step lattice walk in Z^3 with no three collinear vertices
Author: Erik Kalviainen
Public PDF after merge: https://erdos.q5m.io/hilbert-proof.pdf
Source and Lean formalization: https://github.com/ekalvi/erdos-193
Formal theorem: formal/Hilbert193/Hilbert193/Continuity.lean, theorem Hilbert193.erdos193_unconditional

Summary:
Define a nested infinite discrete Hilbert path H:N→N^2. For two Hilbert indices m,n with the same terminal orientation state, the planar chord satisfies an exact two-adic pair law V(H(n)-H(m))=ν₂(|n-m|). A two-base-4-digit correction selects one such index n_a in every aligned block of 16, with 4≤n_{a+1}-n_a≤28. Lift the selected points to P_a=(H(n_a),n_a)∈Z^3. If three lifts were collinear, applying the pair law and its scaling rule to the two adjacent chords and their sum would force two coprime integers to be odd while their sum has two-adic valuation zero, a contradiction. Hilbert adjacency and the bounded index gaps put every consecutive displacement in one fixed finite set.
