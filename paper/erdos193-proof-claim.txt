Proof claim for Erdős Problem #193

Claim: The answer is no. There is a finite set S⊂Z^3 and an infinite S-walk with no three distinct collinear vertices.

Paper: A finite-step lattice walk in Z^3 with no three collinear vertices
Author: Erik Kalviainen
Public PDF after merge: https://erdos-193.q5m.ai/hilbert-proof.pdf
Source and Lean formalization: https://github.com/ekalvi/erdos-193
Formal theorem: formal/Hilbert193/Hilbert193/Continuity.lean, theorem Hilbert193.erdos193_unconditional

Summary:
Define a nested infinite discrete Hilbert path H:N→N² with terminal orientation σ(n). Label the four states cyclically by λ(I),λ(S),λ(C),λ(T)=0,1,2,3 and give them the matching Gray-code corner tags u. Retain every index and define G(n)=2H(n)+u_{σ(n)}, z(n)=4n+λ(σ(n)), and P_n=(G(n),z(n)). The same-state Hilbert pair law and the tag parity cases give the all-pairs identity V(G(n)−G(m))=ν₂(|z(n)−z(m)|). Applying this identity and its scaling rule to two adjacent chords of a hypothetical collinear triple and their sum forces two coprime integers to be odd while their sum is also odd, a contradiction. Hilbert adjacency gives |Δx|,|Δy|≤3, while 1≤Δz≤7, so every consecutive displacement lies in one fixed finite set.
