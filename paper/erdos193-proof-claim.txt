Proof claim for Erdős Problem #193

Claim: The answer is no. There is a finite set S⊂Z^3 and an infinite S-walk with no three distinct collinear vertices.

Paper: A Gaussian-lattice walk in Z^3 with no three collinear vertices
Authors: Erik Kalviainen and Stijn Cambie
Public PDF after merge: https://erdos-193.q5m.ai/gaussian-proof.pdf
Source and Lean formalization: https://github.com/ekalvi/erdos-193
Formal theorem: formal/Hilbert193/Hilbert193/Continuity.lean, theorem Hilbert193.erdos193_unconditional

Summary:
Let u_n=i^{s₂(n)} and z_n=Σ_{r<n}u_r. Binary recursion gives u_{2n+ε}=i^εu_n and z_{2n+ε}=(1+i)z_n+εu_n. Equal direction states yield ν₂(|z_n-z_m|²)=ν₂(n-m) by repeated halving. Matching Gray-code planar tags and base-4 height residues extend the identity to every pair. Applying it to two adjacent chords and their sum excludes a collinear triple. The walk has sixteen state-pair steps with planar coordinates bounded by 2 and height increments from 1 through 7.
